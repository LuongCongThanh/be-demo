# Vietnam e-invoicing obligations for online retail

**Research date / legal snapshot:** 2026-11-21 (Asia/Ho_Chi_Minh)<br>
**Question:** May an online goods retailer issue one end-of-day aggregated e-invoice, or must it invoice individual sales; when, subject to what exceptions, and what should an MVP implement?<br>
**Ticket pointer:** [`docs/plan/wayfinding/fashion-store-mvp-implementation/issues/03-vietnam-e-invoicing-obligations.md`](../docs/plan/wayfinding/fashion-store-mvp-implementation/issues/03-vietnam-e-invoicing-obligations.md)

> **Bottom line.** For ordinary online retail sales of goods, the prudent reading of the current invoice rules is **do not defer separate customer sales into a general end-of-day consolidated invoice**. The seller must issue an invoice when selling goods, and the timing rule is when ownership or the right to use the goods transfers to the buyer, whether or not payment has been received. The cited rules contain no general end-of-day aggregation permission for ordinary goods sales. This conclusion is an interpretation of the combined statutory duty and timing trigger: the decree does not literally say “one invoice per click/order,” and the exact sale/transfer event can depend on the contract and fulfillment model. A single invoice can describe the line items in one sale; the conclusion is not that every SKU or payment authorization needs its own invoice.

## 1. Governing instruments and change history

1. **Decree 123/2020/NĐ-CP** (Government, 19 October 2020) establishes the e-invoice regime. The Government’s record lists its effective date as **1 July 2022**. Relevant provisions are Articles 4 and 9. 2. **Decree 70/2025/NĐ-CP** (Government, 20 March 2025) amends Decree 123, including Article 9 (Article 1(6)) and adds Article 11a on e-invoices generated from cash registers (Article 1(9)). It took effect **1 June 2025**. [Official record](https://vanban.chinhphu.vn/?pageid=27160&docid=213179) · [official signed attachment](https://datafiles.chinhphu.vn/cpp/files/vbpq/2025/3/70-nd-cp.signed.pdf). Accessed 2026-11-21.

## 2. Ordinary goods sales: invoice duty and timing

- **Invoice duty:** Decree 123, Article 4(1), requires a seller to issue an invoice to the buyer when selling goods or providing services, subject to the decree’s specific rules. The obligation is not stated as limited to storefront sales. Read together with Article 9, it applies to the online seller making the taxable sale; a website or marketplace is a sales channel, not a general invoice exemption. (Decree 123, Arts. 4(1), 9(1); as to amendments, Decree 70, Art. 1(6).)
- **Goods timing:** Decree 123, Article 9(1), places the invoice time at the transfer of ownership or the right to use the goods to the buyer, **regardless of whether payment has been received**. Thus, an order timestamp, card capture, or end-of-day settlement is not automatically the legal invoice trigger. For shipped goods, determine the transfer point from the sale terms and actual fulfillment; the decree does not resolve every consumer-delivery or split-shipment contract pattern.
- **No general daily consolidation rule for goods:** Articles 4(1) and 9(1) do not authorize a seller to wait until the end of the day and combine unrelated buyers’ goods sales into one invoice. Article 9(4)(a)’s data-reconciliation timing provision is a narrow rule for qualifying high-volume, recurring **services** that require reconciliation; it is not a general exception for merchandise. Treating a batch of separate customer orders as one sale, or deciding exactly when ownership transfers in a particular online contract, is fact-sensitive and should be confirmed with the seller’s Vietnamese tax authority/adviser.

### Relevant timing exceptions and distinctions

- **Services are different:** Under Decree 123, Article 9(2), a service invoice is generally issued when the service is completed; where payment is collected before or during provision, the invoice time is generally payment receipt, subject to stated exceptions for specified service advances/deposits. These service rules do not postpone the invoice for a normal sale of clothing or other goods.
- **Reconciliation services:** Decree 123, Article 9(4)(a), as amended by Decree 70, Article 1(6), permits the invoice time for specified high-volume, recurring services requiring data reconciliation between seller and buyer/partner to follow reconciliation, but no later than the statutory limit (the seventh day of the following month or seven days after the end of the agreed period, as applicable under the provision). This is conditional and is not a safe basis for ordinary online goods orders merely because a payment processor or marketplace settles in batches.
- **Other sector-specific timing rules:** Article 9 also contains specialized rules for defined activities. They should be checked when the retailer is selling a regulated or specially listed service/product; none identified in the cited provisions creates a general end-of-day exception for ordinary online retail goods.

**Interpretation boundary:** The legal text clearly fixes the goods invoice trigger to transfer of ownership/use right and says payment is immaterial. The operational recommendation to issue per distinct sale/transfer event, rather than one daily invoice for multiple customer sales, follows from the seller-to-buyer obligation plus that timing rule and the absence of a general goods aggregation exception. The provisions do not expressly define “transaction” as checkout, order, package, or payment. An order containing multiple items can be represented as one invoice with multiple lines; a split shipment or delayed acceptance may require contract-specific handling.

## 3. E-commerce and cash-register scope

Decree 123’s general seller duty and goods timing rule are not limited to in-person sales (Arts. 4(1), 9(1)). The official rules reviewed do not create a blanket e-commerce carve-out or a general permission for online retailers to consolidate goods invoices at day end.

Decree 70 adds Article 11a for **e-invoices generated from cash registers connected to data transfer to tax authorities**. Its covered classes include specified business households/individuals (including an annual-revenue condition) and direct-to-consumer businesses in listed activities, including retail other than automobiles, motorcycles and other motor vehicles. (Decree 70, Art. 1(9), inserting Decree 123, Art. 11a.) The legal question is therefore not simply “online or offline”: the seller’s legal form, activity classification, revenue/status conditions and actual direct-to-consumer model matter. The text does not state that being online alone excludes a retailer from “retail,” nor that every online seller automatically falls into this cash-register regime. Confirm Article 11a applicability and compliant issuance method for the actual seller with the competent tax authority or Vietnamese tax adviser.

Article 11a is an invoice-generation/connection regime for qualifying sellers; it is **not** a general authority to combine unrelated sales into one daily invoice. Whether the MVP seller is the merchant of record, an agent, or an online marketplace operator must also be settled separately: do not infer that marketplace settlement or tax withholding by a platform transfers or removes the underlying seller’s invoice duty without a specific legal basis.

## 4. Practical MVP compliance integration

The following are implementation recommendations based on the statutory obligations above, not additional statutory text:

1. **Resolve seller and tax profile before launch.** Record the invoice-issuing legal entity/household, tax identification and tax regime, seller-of-record role, direct-to-consumer activity, and the facts needed to assess Article 11a (including revenue/category where relevant). Get local confirmation for a fashion e-commerce seller’s classification and any platform/agency arrangement.
2. **Model a legally meaningful sale/transfer event.** Keep order acceptance, payment, dispatch, delivery/acceptance, and ownership-transfer timestamps distinct. Configure the invoice trigger to the documented contractual transfer point; do not hard-code “daily close” or use payment settlement as a substitute for the Article 9(1) trigger. Store the reason/event that caused issuance, in Vietnam local time.
3. **Issue against the sale, not the SKU.** Support one invoice containing multiple goods lines for a single sale, but do not roll multiple customers’ ordinary goods sales into an end-of-day invoice absent written confirmation of a specific statutory basis. Make the invoice idempotently link to the order/sale and the relevant fulfillment event.
4. **Use the applicable e-invoice path.** Integrate with a compliant e-invoice provider/tax-authority workflow for the seller’s regime. If Article 11a applies, implement the required cash-register-generated format and tax-authority data connection rather than assuming a generic invoice endpoint suffices. Preserve the issued invoice and its status, and provide it to the buyer through the selected delivery channel.
5. **Keep correction and audit trails.** Do not delete/rewrite an issued invoice on cancellation, return, failed delivery, or pricing correction. Implement the applicable statutory correction/replacement process (Decree 123, Article 19, as amended by Decree 70) with references back to the original invoice and order. Log provider responses, retries, tax-authority transmission and buyer delivery. Retry/error handling is an operational control, not a license to delay past the legal issue time.
6. **Keep service exceptions separate.** If the business later sells services, use separate event logic for service completion, advance payments and any demonstrably qualifying reconciliation arrangement. Obtain tax review before using Article 9(4)(a); routine marketplace or payment settlement alone is not proof that its conditions are met.

## 5. Questions to close before production

- What is the legal entity that sells the goods and whose tax ID/name appears as seller to the customer? Is the platform only a channel, an agent, or the seller?
- Under the customer terms, at what precise event does ownership/use right transfer for shipped, split, refused, returned or cash-on-delivery orders?
- Does this seller fall within Article 11a, including the direct-consumer retail description and applicable legal-form/revenue conditions? If yes, what exact cash-register-generated invoice implementation has the tax office accepted for its online operations?
- Which compliant e-invoice provider/system, invoice type and data-transmission route applies to the seller’s tax regime? What is the prescribed handling for outages and invoice corrections under the seller’s circumstances?
